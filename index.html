Case file / 01 Start — City Funding Fundamentals

City Council discretionary funds are municipal grants allocated by Council Members to non-profits operating free public programs in their districts. Not a loan. Never paid back.

What is Discretionary Funding?

Municipal grants awarded directly by New York City Council Members to local 501(c)(3) charities and community organizations. Grants fund youth tutoring, food pantries, senior wellness, cultural arts, and park cleanups.

Why does OneNYC do this for $0 fee?

We are a civic coalition of non-profit leaders and administrative specialists. We believe grassroots organizations doing ground-level neighborhood work should have equal access to municipal tax dollars without paying private consultants $3,000–$5,000.

How much can our charity receive?

First-time awards typically range from $10,000 to $25,000 per Council Member, with total allocations reaching $50,000 across multiple members. Funds go directly into your organization's bank account.

Case file / 02 Compliance — Pre-Audit Protocol

Most first applications do not fail on program merits. They fail on technical compliance: a lapsed state registration, an uncollected board resolution, or mismatched fiscal year figures. We pre-audit every line before submission.

NYS Charities Bureau Registration

Your CHAR500 filing must be active and current on the exact date of submission. We verify state database records in advance and resolve any administrative gaps.

Board Resolutions & Notarizations

City rules require a formal board authorization signed by an officer and notarized. We supply approved templates and guide your board through execution.

Independent Organization EIN

Council grants require direct incorporation under your own Tax ID (EIN). Fiscal sponsorships are not permitted under Council rules. We confirm your direct standing.

Municipal Fiscal Alignment

NYC operating budgets run July 1 through June 30. We format your program budget to match City Council cost categories and administrative caps.

Case file / 03 Eligibility — The Five Qualification Checks

Does your charity qualify? If you operate free public programs serving NYC residents, you likely meet all city standards. Here are the five objective checks:

01. 501(c)(3) or NY Non-Profit

Registered as a non-profit corporation in New York State or holding IRS 501(c)(3) tax-exempt status.

02. Free Public Programming

Programs must be free to participants and open to the public in NYC neighborhoods (youth, food, seniors, arts, wellness).

03. Dedicated EIN

Your organization holds its own Federal Employer Identification Number (not through a fiscal sponsor).

04. State Charities Registration

Active registration with the New York State Attorney General's Charities Bureau, or formal documentation of exemption.

05. Authorized Signer

A designated director, officer, or executive ready to sign grant agreements and board disclosures.

Case file / 04 The Packet — The 13 Required City Filings

Thirteen documents, four groups. Assembled once and submitted directly across your targeted Council Members.

Legal Group (4 Documents)

Certificate of Incorporation (NYS DOS), IRS EIN Confirmation, 501(c)(3) Federal Determination, and NYS Charities Bureau registration.

Financial Group (4 Documents)

IRS Form 990 / 990-EZ, latest CHAR500 filing, Certified Financial Audit or CPA Review, and Annual Operating Budget.

Governance Group (3 Documents)

Active Board of Directors Roster with professional affiliations, Organizational By-Laws with Conflict of Interest policy, and Board Resolution.

Insurance & City Forms (2 Documents)

Commercial General Liability ($1M+ naming City of New York as additional insured), and Workers' Compensation (C-105.2 / DB-120.1) or exemption.
