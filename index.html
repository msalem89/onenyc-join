Your organization must have

1
Nonprofit incorporation
Incorporated as a not-for-profit. 501(c)(3) is most common; some other 501(c) types may also qualify.
2
Its own EIN
A federal tax ID in your organization's own name. The Council does not allow fiscal sponsors or using another group's EIN.
3
Charities Bureau registration
Current registration with the NYS Attorney General's Charities Bureau, with annual filings up to date, or a documented exemption.
4
An authorized officer
A board member or executive who can sign for the organization and get a form notarized.
5
A clean record
No unresolved problems with City agencies, and willingness to disclose past Council funding and any conflicts of interest.
2

Your program must be

City funding pays for a public service, not for an organization's religious or member activities. Masajid and faith-based groups are welcome to apply for programs that serve the whole community.

ELIGIBLE PROGRAMS
  • Free to participants
  • Open to the public, including non-members
  • Tutoring, homework help, ESL
  • Youth sports and mentoring (outside school hours)
  • Food access, health, senior services
  • Arts and culture open to all
NOT ELIGIBLE
  • Worship or prayer services
  • Religious instruction (e.g. Quran classes)
  • Members-only activities
  • Anything that promotes a religion
  • Political activity, campaigns or lobbying
  • Programs during school hours, unless arranged through the DOE with the principal's consent
A school or program under your masjid? If it uses the masjid's EIN, it can't apply on its own: either the masjid applies for a clearly public program it runs, or the school incorporates and gets its own EIN. We'll help you choose.

Good to know before you apply
  • Keep the program narrow. The City funds specific, single-issue programs with clear start and end dates — not a broad mission. A focused program gets funded; "we do everything" does not.
  • Apply to more than one Council Member. The caps are per Council Member and each application is separate — most partners target at least two offices. Some offices also ask for a short supplemental one-pager describing your work in their district; we'll help you build it.

Before OneNYC · Grassroots Friction

The Solo Non-Profit Burden

  • The Pitching Tax: Separate manual applications to every individual Council Member; booking dozens of meetings with zero dedicated grant staff.
  • The Holistic Trap: Funders want narrow, single-issue projects with rigid start/end dates. Holistic organizations providing housing, job support, and mental health get excluded.
  • Retracted Commitments: Council members verbally promised funding, met twice, and later pulled it ("no more money available"). Core services paused in May.
  • Disillusionment: Small grassroots orgs give up entirely on municipal funding cycles due to administrative burnout.
With OneNYC · Coalition Model

  • Modular Program Packaging: Translates holistic community services into compliant, fundable City Council program modules without altering your mission.
  • Citywide Slate Representation: Applying as part of an organized multi-borough coalition provides collective legislative standing and prevents single-member cuts.
  • Full Non-Profit Autonomy: Your organization remains the direct applicant and contract holder, retaining 100% of awarded grant funds.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the OneNYC Impact Partner program?

OneNYC helps community organizations prepare strong applications for New York City Council discretionary funding, which opens each January. We handle the paperwork and compliance so you can focus on the public service your program provides. Most first awards are $10,000 to $20,000 for a local program.

Who can apply?

Your organization needs: (1) nonprofit incorporation (501(c)(3) is most common), (2) its own federal EIN, (3) current NYS Charities Bureau registration, (4) an authorized officer who can sign and get a form notarized, and (5) a clean record with City agencies. Masajid and faith-based groups are welcome to apply for programs that serve the whole community.

What kinds of programs qualify?

Programs that are free to participants and open to the public: tutoring, homework help, ESL, youth sports and mentoring outside school hours, food access, health, senior services, and arts and culture open to all. Worship services, religious instruction, members-only activities, and political activity do not qualify.

What documents do I need?

Thirteen essential filings are listed in the Documents section above — use the interactive readiness checklist to see where you stand before you begin intake. Every item is required per organization: if two related groups apply (for example, a masjid and its school), each needs its own complete set.

Are there funding limits for newer organizations?

Yes. If your organization was legally created within the last two fiscal years: up to $10,000 per Council Member and $20,000 total, with no subcontractors. If it has not received Council funding in the last three fiscal years: up to $25,000 per Council Member and $50,000 total. These are limits, not bans — the Council can waive them case by case.

Can we apply to more than one Council Member?

Yes — and you should. Caps are per Council Member, each application is separate, and most partners target at least two offices. Some offices ask for a short supplemental one-pager on your work in their district; we help you prepare it.

What is the deadline?

Return your intake form — starting with the signed mutual non-disclosure agreement — to OneNYC by November 15, 2026. The Council application is expected to open in early January and close in mid-February.

What are the intake steps?

Four steps: (1) execute the standard 2-party mutual NDA, (2) upload your documents and complete City municipal readiness, (3) capacity and alignment review, and (4) workspace access for your application. Begin below.

I still have questions.

Email us at info@onenyc.com — if you're missing something from the requirements, talk to us anyway: many gaps can be fixed before the deadline.
